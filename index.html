About

I am an economist and policy researcher at the Development Policy Research Unit (DPRU) in the School of Economics at the University of Cape Town, where I am a Junior Research Fellow. I am also an Affiliate Researcher in the Global Researcher Network of the Anker Research Institute.

My research examines how institutions and public policy shape labour market opportunities and the quality of work, and how these processes contribute to poverty and inequality. I am particularly interested in the relationship between labour market institutions, employment conditions and workers' economic security.

My current research focuses on job quality, wages and living standards, social protection, changing forms of employment and employment policy in South Africa. I work primarily with household survey and administrative data and have contributed to living wage estimates using the Anker methodology.

My work combines applied economic research with policy analysis, with contributions to research undertaken for government departments, policy institutions and international organisations. I hold Honours and Master's degrees in Economic Science from the University of the Witwatersrand.

Research

Research agenda

My research asks whether work in South Africa provides a decent and secure living, and how institutions and public policy shape the answer. At its core is a connected set of questions: how good jobs are and how job quality can be measured, whether wages are enough to meet the cost of a decent standard of living, and who is protected by social security when work does not provide enough. I approach these questions within the wider setting of labour market institutions and employment policy, including minimum wages, employment incentives and regulation, and with attention to what they mean for poverty and inequality.

Most of this work is empirical, drawing on household surveys, administrative data and primary data collection, and much of it is undertaken with colleagues at the DPRU and with partners in government and research institutions. My current work extends these questions to new forms of employment, such as platform work, and to how the cost of a decent living varies across regions of South Africa. The five themes below organise this work.

Research themes

  • Labour Markets and Employment PolicyHow employment and unemployment evolve, how labour market institutions and regulation shape them, and what employment incentives and wider employment policy can achieve.
  • Job Quality and Decent WorkWhat makes a job good or poor, how job quality can be measured with the data available, and how working conditions differ across formal, informal and platform work.
  • Wages and Living StandardsWhat workers earn compared with what it costs to live decently, including minimum wages, living wage benchmarks and how earnings change over time.
  • Social Protection and Economic SecurityWho is covered by social security and who is left out, and how gaps in protection leave workers and households exposed to risk.
  • Institutions, Poverty and InequalityHow the design and functioning of public institutions shape economic opportunity and distributional outcomes. This includes my earlier work on crime and inequality in South Africa, and on the structure and financing of local government.

Related interests include skills and labour demand, and the just transition.
